About this survey

How would you prefer to pay for treatment?

We're always working to improve how we support our patients and make treatment more accessible. This short survey helps us understand what matters most to you when it comes to paying for treatment.

On the next screens we'll show you different ways of paying and ask which you'd choose. It takes about five minutes, there are no right answers, and taking part commits you to nothing.

Leadership: patient-facing framing kept general (improving support), not policy-specific. Same instrument in both frames; only the sample and the "do nothing" option change.

Before you choose

How the Smile Loan would work

The treatment is the same in every case — a full-jaw dental implant solution priced at £16,995. What changes is how you pay for it.

A Smile Loan

A possible new payment option
  • No depositYou start treatment without paying anything up front.
  • You repayA set share of what you earn from work, and only while you earn above a set amount.
  • Your pension is safeNothing is ever taken from your state or private pension — repayments come only from money you earn from working.
  • It ends when you stopWhen you stop working or retire, repayments stop. Anything still owing is written off — and always cancelled on death.
In the choices, the Smile Loan's repayment share, threshold and any upfront fee will change from one option to the next. Everything on this page stays the same.
Leadership: the write-off is now stated as a fixed feature, not a lever — because repayments are earned-income-only, retirement already stops them and the write-off age is nearly cost-neutral for this cohort. Variation is on the levers that actually bite: rate, threshold, fee.

What this delivers (leadership view)

Demand curveHow uptake falls as terms get harsher — so we can set the least generous terms that still hit target volume, minimising the subsidy. This is the number the Treasury cares about.
SubstitutionFrom the in-clinic frame: the share who'd switch off "pay it myself now" onto a loan. Replaces the 15% deadweight assumption.
Induced demandFrom the Facebook-group frame (opt-out = "stay as I am"): the share of priced-out prospects who convert. Replaces the "500 new patients" guess.
Hidden borrowingFrom the funding question: how much "cash" is really credit-card / family debt.
External checkAgainst the real 9.9% plan: predicted uptake should track observed take-up of that plan.
